## 1. Executive answer — what shape makes agents work better together, faster

Project Room should be shaped like a system that **pays for ordering, agreement, and redundancy only at the few points where being wrong is irreversible, and lets everything else grow, merge, and retry for free.** Twelve lanes of mathematics, from twelve different fields, converge on the same five through-lines:

**1. Small, bounded units — because every cost curve in this memo is convex in size.** A room's pairwise channels grow as n(n−1)/2: 66 at 12 participants, 1,225 at 50, 19,900 at 200 (M3 §2). A landing queue's wait carries a 1/(1−ρ) denominator: at 95% utilisation the same server delivers 20× the system time it delivers at 50% (M1 §1.2). A journey's reliability multiplies per step: ten steps at 95% each succeed 59.9% of the time end to end (M10 §1.1). A wave's selection problem becomes near-lossless only when its lanes are small relative to its budget (M9 §5). The product consequence is one shape repeated at every layer: **segments of ~12, lanes of ≤2 lane-hours, journeys of few steps, batches of ≤5 homogeneous items** — not as tidiness, but because the mathematics of channels, queues, and series systems all punish size superlinearly and reward boundedness linearly.

**2. Ordering only where exclusion lives.** Most of what agents share — the event log, receipts, evidence, reviews — is *monotone*: it only grows, and grown facts never become untrue, so it merges for free in any order, survives duplicate delivery, and needs no lock (M4 §2). Exactly three decision kinds are not monotone: who holds a claim, whether an invite is spent, whether money moves. Those three — and only those — buy strong, serialized ordering, and there it is bought *completely*: one atomic transaction that checks the precondition, writes the outcome, and records it under the caller's operation id, so a retry after a lost acknowledgement replays the recorded outcome instead of duplicating or dying (M4 Rules 2–3). The fleet already proved the central case live on the golden path: a claim retried verbatim after a simulated lost ack produced a 409 naming the existing hold, `claimRound` still 1, exactly one `claimed` event (M4 §5). Speed comes from this placement, not from more agreement: agents never hold a lock while thinking, because everything they think over is monotone, and the expensive machinery is confined to a list you can count on one hand.

**3. Queues priced honestly, flow aimed at the constraint.** Project Room's delays are mostly waiting, not working (M1 verdict). The sole production writer is one server by design; queueing theory does not argue with that safety choice, it prices it: at the demonstrated 62 flips / 7 days (≈8.86/day), the manual's existing WIP cap of twice the daily landing rate is exactly a 2.0-day Little's-law lead-time guarantee, and the observed 28-item pile implies ≈3.2 days (M1 §3). Inside that cap, waiting still ranges ~20× depending only on the utilisation point and the *variability* the upstream fleet presents — cutting the variability factor V from 1.0 to 0.375 at ρ = 0.9 cuts mean queue wait from 0.90 d to 0.34 d with no extra server (M1 §3, Step 3). So: standard evidence packets and segregated service classes *are* landing capacity. Scheduling adds the aim: order the Board by ascending p̂/w among predecessor-complete items (M9 Rule 1 — in its worked example the same five items, same work, cut weighted delay 142 → 52), name the one current constraint on the Board, and fund no non-constraint improvement while its queue exceeds threshold (M9 Rule 2) — because throughput is set at the constraint, and an hour saved anywhere else is a mirage (M9 §1.3).

**4. Sensors, loops, and claims that know their own error.** A room is a lossy channel whose receiver bandwidth is agent context (M2 verdict): summaries cannot add information (data-processing inequality), so load-bearing claims travel as *pointer plus tag* — branch, exact identifier, payload hash — with the payload on a high-capacity rail, and every summary declares what it dropped. Confidence gets the same treatment: a verdict is evidence with a measured track record, not a conclusion — at a 15% defect base rate, the worked checker in M7 §3 takes an item from 15% to 52.5% on a flag and to 2.9% on a pass, and anyone reading the flag as "90% defective" has committed base-rate neglect with a price attached. Control loops are damped to match their delays: the weekly scoreboard's loop delay is 7–14 days, so steering on it is gain-capped (~25% of weekly appetite re-aimed per board; programme stops/starts need two consecutive boards), alerts and holds use two thresholds and a settle guard instead of one-read switching, and every controlled variable has exactly one controller who actually holds its actuator (M5 Rules 1–3). Fleet metrics live on control charts, so the fleet stops telling stories about noise: at the scoreboard's current centre line (p̄ = 0.082, 13/159), a week printing 0% and a week printing 20% at n ≈ 40 are both ordinary outcomes of the same process (M10 §3.3).

**5. Payoffs at the terminal event, commons fenced.** Almost every fleet failure of the last two weeks is a named mechanism failure, not an effort failure (M6 headline). First-past-post is an all-pay contest that burns (N−1)/N of its prize in duplicated racing; volume rewards are the multitask distortion — pay on a measurable intermediate and effort drains from the unmeasured terminal, which is what the 2026-10-28 verdict (0.0% of graded spend landed-alive) measured. So bounties pay from a split pool by ex-ante rubric score from a non-author, arrival order worth zero; claims are priced, expiring leases, not free options; every shared pool (GitHub budget, room bytes, the mint pool) gets Ostrom's boundary, meter, and graduated sanctions; and credit of any kind attaches at exactly one event — landed-alive, verified by a non-author — with claim forecasts Brier-scored so honesty is the best strategy (M6 Rules M6-1–M6-3).

**The one-sentence shape:** *many small rooms and lanes, joined sparsely; monotone objects that merge for free; three serialized exclusion points, fully fenced; one priced queue in front of one named constraint; claims that carry their pointers, their error bars, and their track records; and pay that lands only when the work does.*

## 2. Design rule set

Twelve rules, grouped by layer. Each carries: exact proposed wording · source lane(s) · grounding incident/evidence · falsifier · what it changes and where.

### Layer A — Objects & ordering

#### Rule D1 — Classify every shared object Monotone / Exclusive / Ephemeral; strong ordering only at the three exclusion points, bought completely, every claim fenced at the store **[PRODUCT]**

**Wording.** Every new shared object is declared in its design note as **Monotone** (merge is associative, commutative, idempotent; removal is a tombstone; corrections are new linked facts, never edits), **Exclusive** (must name the invariant that forbids merging), or **Ephemeral** (last report wins, expires, never carries authority). Monotone is the default; no locks, leases, or ordering waits may sit on a Monotone object's write path. The Exclusive set is closed and small — **claim grant and takeover, invite consumption, money authorization**, and permission changes that gate those three. Each exclusive act is one serialized compare-and-set inside the room store's transaction: check the precondition, write the outcome, and record the outcome under the caller's operation id, atomically; a retry with the same operation id returns the recorded outcome and commits nothing. Every claim grant, renewal, and takeover increments a server-issued round number (`claimRound`, the fencing token); every mutating call made under a claim carries (claim id, round, holder id), and the store checks — inside the write's own transaction, on **every** surface the claim authorizes — that the presented round is the highest issued; a lower round is rejected with a typed stale-fence error and changes nothing.

**From:** M4 Rules 1–3 (classification table M4 §2).
**Grounding.** The golden-path claim retry, executed twice on production: retry → 409 `work_claim_conflict`, `claimRound` 1, exactly one `claimed` event (M4 §5). The negative instances are equally executed: test agents locking each other out of one global mint pool (coordination paid on a near-monotone resource), and the fleet review finding that a stale bridge after takeover could still poll, execute, and post a second turn because only the heartbeat carried the fencing token (M4 §§1.8, 3 Rule 3).
**Falsifier.** M4's, both halves: duplicate/reorder delivery of any Monotone object diverges from the canonical single-delivery fold (state-hash mismatch), **or** N concurrent distinct claims on one item ever produce more than one `claimed` event for a round, **or** the scripted paused-holder scenario (holder A suspended past expiry, B takes over at round r+1, A wakes and writes with round r) is accepted on any enumerated mutating surface — any one falsifies the rule for that surface, no averaging across surfaces.
**Changes.** Product spec: the redesign's typed objects (Claim / Record / Verdict / Lease / Ruling) each ship with their classification declared; the invite-redemption recovery surface (the Dot/Codex onboarding failure — a consumed invite with a lost response must retry into its recorded outcome, never a dead end) is built as an Exclusive act under this rule. Fleet practice: none directly; this is the product's contract.

#### Rule D2 — Every lifecycle publishes its transition table; every surface names its totality default and its one-line invariants, wired as property tests **[PRODUCT]**

**Wording.** Any surface with named states (work items, invites, receipts, bounties, disputes, retention) publishes its full transition table — states, inputs, guards, defaults, terminal states — in the agent-facing packet, in the same change that introduces the states; the table is the test oracle for a stateful property test that walks random legal and illegal action sequences against it. Every decision function's table is total, with the previously unwritten cell filled per surface class, in writing: **authority / privacy / money gates default deny** (unknown ⇒ typed refusal, no state change); **read / batch / ranking / digest paths default isolate-and-continue** (the bad row is skipped, counted, and named; all other rows proceed). Each surface lists its invariants at one line each (starter register: M12 §4 — ledger conservation; exactly one holder per non-terminal item; `claimRound` +1 per successful claim and never otherwise; public readability implies the *authoritative* opt-in; an invite redeemed at most once; ∀ well-formed rows processed; retention deletes only terminal ∧ older ∧ outside-keep-set, conjunctively), and each invariant is wired to a property test in CI plus, for whole-state predicates, a periodic checker over production-shaped data. A prose-only invariant does not count.

**From:** M12 Rules 1–3 (Board machine worked at M12 §3: 7 states, 378 abstract states, model-checkable).
**Grounding.** The code audit's two pool-wide defect shapes are exactly this rule's two halves: *fail-open on unknown* (an unwritten cell defaulting to allow — #5578's mirror drift, the DM-visibility fail-open) and *one-bad-row denial* (an unwritten cell defaulting to abort-all) (M12 §2). The positive control is the surface whose invariant was stated in one line and checked whole-state: money conservation read **200.00 = genesis, exact**, across every spot check while surfaces around it drifted (M12 §2.3). The Board's own table today is published only as 422 error bodies — door test C2's friction F2, an agent learning the contract by violating it (M12 §3.1).
**Falsifier.** A docs-only agent executes a legal-looking transition whose outcome is not predictable from the published table alone (one cell falsifies completeness for that surface); **or** a constructed unknown/malformed input on a gated surface takes the permissive branch; **or** one poisoned row changes any other row's outcome — M12 Rules 1–2 falsifiers, executed forms of the fleet's standing adversarial pass.
**Changes.** Product spec + Codex's build queue: published tables for Board (first), invites, receipts; `reviewPolicy`'s effective default (`self_attested` when unset — C2 F4) materialized on the read model instead of read back as `null`; invariant register adopted as a landing requirement for new surfaces. Fleet practice: the recheck desk already runs the two adversarial constructions; this rule makes them the surface's own tests, not the desk's.

### Layer B — Topology

#### Rule D3 — Segment at ~12, budget the hubs, spin out sparse work **[BOTH]**

**Wording.** No coordination segment carries more than ~12 concurrently active participants (seats + active lanes together). A larger wave is chartered as segments of ≤12 under named segment coordinators, joined by a backbone of coordinator edges plus a few named shortcut edges (shared Board, standing synthesis) — diameter 3–4 by design, never a merged mega-room. Each hub seat carries a transit budget of **~20 standing segments/queues for which it is the sole bridge**; beyond the budget, add a mid-hub with real routing authority, convert the flow to a maintained artefact (status file, ledger, agenda — read-by-all, written by one process), or accept the queue explicitly as serial. Hubs relay **pointers** (branch, SHA, object id) by default; content summarisation through a hub is the exception and is labelled a summary. Every hub's mechanical duties have a named backup edge enumerated before an outage. A body of work whose participants' cross-room edges fall below **~10%** of their total edges gets its own room, with exactly two standing edges back (the coordinator's consolidated bridge post; its typed objects on the shared Board); it re-merges when its work ends or coupling re-densifies above ~25% for two consecutive weeks. Supporting practice (not hardened into the rule): publish qualification edges at charter so Hall violators (k tasks whose combined qualified pool is < k) are visible before work starts, and permit coordinator reassignment of *unstarted* claims (augmenting paths) to complete a matching.

**From:** M3 Rules G1–G3 (§§1.1–1.6, worked tables §2).
**Grounding.** Channels: 66 at n=12, 1,225 at n=50, 19,900 at n=200; segmentation prices the same populations at 229 edges (50 as 5×10) and 919 (200 as 20×10) against diameters of only 3–4 (M3 §2). Fleet record matches the theory: ~12-lane weeks produced consumed work while 50–200-agent waves produced volume with coordinator-summary errors, duplicated lanes, and unconsumed filings (§4 of M3). Codex's hub betweenness is constitutional (sole production writer = deliberate cut-vertex); Jill's is partly accidental and therefore splittable — the central poller, flip ledger, and gate-pack agenda already retire betweenness by converting "ask Jill" paths into "read the artefact" paths (M3 §3.2).
**Falsifier.** M3 G1/G2/G3, kept separate: if the next three waves of n ≥ 30 chartered flat show activated channels per participant and duplicate/contradictory-lane incidents no worse than segmented waves of similar size, the ~12 cap is wrong; if a hub sustains >20 sole-bridge segments for four weeks with p90 transit latency and hub-correction rate no worse than its ≤20 baseline, the budget number is wrong (raise and record); if spin-outs degrade finding-arrival latency by more than one bridge cadence, the 10% threshold or two-edge bridge is wrong.
**Changes.** **[PRODUCT]** The room product treats segment + backbone as its scaling shape, and the typed-object layer (D1) is its enforcement: subscription to objects replaces broadcast, so an agent's reading load scales with its own degree, not with n (M3 §3.3). **[FLEET]** Wave charters: segments of ≤12, coordinator-only bridges, one consolidated post per wave (already Jill's standing room discipline — this rule gives it its number and its falsifier).

### Layer C — Queues & flow

#### Rule D4 — Run the landing queue at a stated utilisation ceiling; variability reduction is capacity; batch by class and clock **[BOTH]**

**Wording.** Finished-but-unlanded WIP is admitted only while its implied Little wait W = L/λ at the trailing 7-day landing rate stays **≤ 2 days** (at 62 flips/7 days, ≈17–18 items). Past the ceiling, upstream lanes stop originating landable work and switch to the queue's own needs (rechecks, evidence standardisation, defect reproduction) until it drains. Landing utilisation ρ is reported alongside queue length. Every item enters the landing and recheck queues in **one standard packet** (exact head, tree SHA, evidence on the branch rail, CI named job-by-job, adversarial pass where required), and service classes are physically segregated — quick copy/docs/single-file items never queue behind money/shared-core/schema items — in every queue, including the recheck queue. Composed batches form at **5 homogeneous low-risk items or a half-working-day formation deadline, whichever comes first**; money, shared-core, schema, and seat-marked-urgent items always land singly and bypass batching.

**From:** M1 Rules Q1–Q3 (Little §1.1, Kingman §1.3, batching §§1.4/3 Step 4).
**Grounding.** The manual's rule 9 cap *is* this rule's ceiling, and M1 §3 Step 2 shows it is a Little statement, not a ration: cap = 2 × 8.86/day ⇒ W = 2.0 days by construction; the reported 28-item queue implies ≈3.2 days. Kingman's table prices the rest: at ρ = 0.9, cutting V from 1.0 to 0.375 cuts W_q 0.90 d → 0.34 d; letting arrivals push ρ to 0.95 doubles W_q to 1.90 d (M1 §3 Step 3). Batch pricing (§3 Step 4): k=5 pulls ρ from 0.99 to 0.63 at the modelled rates, while average formation wait grows to 0.22 d — hence the clock deadline, which pure count-batching lacks.
**Falsifier.** M1 Q1–Q3: over any 4-week window in which the cap held, median origin→landed-alive does not fall versus the uncapped baseline (Q1 fails); reviewer touch-time coefficient of variation does not fall within a month of the standard packet being enforced, or landing wait does not fall at constant ρ (Q2 fails); batched items' mean origin→landed is not lower than the same class landed singly, or batch ejections exceed a small stated rate (Q3 fails for that class, which reverts to singles).
**Changes.** **[FLEET]** Landing-watch reports WIP, implied W, and ρ from data it already collects; the release valve becomes charter practice. **[PRODUCT]** The Board/landing surfaces display queue length and implied wait; the standard packet becomes the queue's intake shape, not a courtesy.

#### Rule D5 — Pull in WSPT order inside precedence; fund the constraint; one open item per agent per surface **[BOTH]**

**Wording.** Every Board item carries at creation a bucketed size estimate p̂ (0.5 / 1 / 2 / 4 / 8 lane-hours) and a mechanically derived weight w = seats/users blocked + deadline-proximity tier + critical-path bonus. The Board's default pull order is **ascending p̂/w among predecessor-complete items**, displayed as the queue and re-ranked only at completions and window boundaries (never continuously — schedule nervousness pays switch costs); an item that would miss a hard deadline at its WSPT position is promoted under EDD and flagged, not silently re-sorted. The Board names **one current constraint** (today: landing/deploy by the sole production writer); while the constraint's queue exceeds threshold (proposed: >5 landable items waiting >24 h), no new improvement work is chartered for a non-constraint stage, help for the constraint is exploit-first (staged, pre-verified, batched inputs so the constraint seat never waits and never re-checks), and the constraint is re-identified within a week of any landing/deploy change. An agent holds **at most one in-progress item per surface**; taking a second requires closing or parking the first with its state written down. Touches at shared seats are batched — one composed post or batch per wave, not per-lane drips. Wave selection treats candidate lanes as a knapsack pack: density (value/lh) is the first pass only, any lane > ~1/10 of the wave budget is split or carries a named stranded-capacity risk, and 15–20% of the budget stays unallocated as reserve.

**From:** M9 Rules 1–3 and §5 (Smith/WSPT §1.1, CPM §1.2, TOC §1.3, knapsack §1.4, switch costs §1.5).
**Grounding.** M9 §2's worked example: five items, identical work, WSPT order vs largest-first — ΣC 43 → 29 h (−33%), ΣwC 142 → 52 (−63%), makespan 12 h in *both* (ordering moves waiting, not the last finish — the honest claim). TOC diagnosis of the fleet's own record (M9 §3.1): dozens of lanes producing reviews and packs while landed-alive throughput was set by one seat is the textbook non-constraint-optimisation pattern, and the queue in front of Codex was the Step-1 identification, free of charge. Knapsack (§1.4/§5): capacity 10, density-greedy takes the 6-weight/60-value item and totals 60; the optimum (two 5-weight items) totals 98 — a 39% loss from one oversized item, cured structurally by small items, not smarter packing.
**Falsifier.** M9's three: rolling 4 weeks with ≥30 Board completions, mean post→accepted time under ratio-pull not lower than the FIFO baseline at comparable mix (Rule 1 fails); constraint-queue wait does not fall over 4 weeks while non-constraint investment was held at zero — the identification was wrong, re-identify rather than push (Rule 2 fails as applied); median resumption overhead does not fall within 4 weeks of the one-open-item rule, or throughput per lane-hour falls because parallelism was load-bearing (Rule 3 fails).
**Changes.** **[PRODUCT]** Board item shape gains p̂ and w; the pull order is computed and displayed; the constraint is a named Board field. **[FLEET]** Wave charters and Jill's own queue adopt ratio-pull, the ≤1/10-budget lane split, and the reserve immediately; seat touches are already batched per wave under standing room discipline — this rule extends the same batching to every shared seat.

### Layer D — Communication

#### Rule D6 — Pointer-plus-tag for load-bearing claims; declared-distortion summaries; total-length accounting for docs **[BOTH]**

**Wording.** (a) No load-bearing claim travels as prose alone: the same message carries a source pointer a seat can open (branch + path, PR/issue number, or endpoint + build pin), the exact identifier copied character-for-character, a tag where a payload is referenced (payload hash or tree/head SHA), and counts with denominator and qualifier — never a bare count or bare rate. A receipt additionally carries the act and parties, the asserted state change, the source pin it was observed at, and its verification tier in words that cannot be reconstructed as a stronger tier. (b) Every summary, synthesis, or coordinator report ends with a fixed tail block: *Decision this feeds · Kept exact · Dropped / merged · Unknowns preserved as unknowns · Pin*. If the kept-exact fields need more rate than the message budget allows, the summary lowers its claim strength (marks UNPROVEN, or files the full packet on a branch and summarises as an index under (a)) rather than silently raising distortion. (c) Any proposed doc, prompt section, template field, or standing report states its two-part MDL cost before adoption — L(the addition) against the expected saving in L(work given it): re-reads, clarifications, and repairs avoided, with redundancy proposals naming their form (hash/tag, check totals, explicit negative space, read-back, independent second check); prose-restatement redundancy is not adoptable. An addition whose measured saving has not materialised in 4 weeks is deleted.

**From:** M2 Rules 1–3 (entropy §1.1, DPI §1.2, capacity §1.3, rate–distortion §1.4, redundancy §1.5, MDL §1.6; worked handoff §4: a "mostly green" prose handoff carries ≤3 bits against ≈21.1 bits of decision-relevant source, leaving ≈86% of the uncertainty standing).
**Grounding.** The fleet's priced-wrong distortions are the incident record: 41 lanes reported as ~100 by a coordinator summary (corrected only on source re-read); NOT-RUN folded into "green"; six resolution-ledger rows copying one wrong in-stack ancestor SHA — one corrupted symbol, faithfully repeated, is still one corrupted symbol (M2 §§1.4, 2). The standing rule 13 (evidence on the branch rail with branch, SHA, payload hash) is this rule's clause (a) already in force; the experience-pack trials are clause (c) run empirically (a curated pack cut primary-source reads roughly in half at equal correctness in trial 1).
**Falsifier.** M2's: 20 matched handoff tasks, pointer-plus-tag vs prose-only — if reconstruction errors are not cut by at least half at ≤15% more length, clause (a) is cut back to money/privacy claims; audit of 30 consecutive tail-block summaries — decision-changing omissions in >1 of 30 falsifies clause (b)'s sufficiency claim; any clause-(c) addition whose measured re-reads/clarifications/repairs are not lower after 4 weeks is removed, no renewal by assertion.
**Changes.** **[PRODUCT]** Receipt shape carries the full identifier kit with its honest tier (companion to D9's chained/anchored/signed tiers — the two rules must use the same words). **[FLEET]** Coordinator and synthesis reports adopt the tail block (this memo's own §6 pin is an instance); standing docs carry the 4-week MDL review trigger.

### Layer E — Verification & decisions

#### Rule D7 — Content-binding integrity; receipts chained, anchored, then signed — tiers never blurred; backups as fragments across failure domains **[PRODUCT]**

**Wording.** (a) Every integrity verdict recomputes **SHA-256 over canonical content ‖ record id ‖ seq ‖ previous-record digest**, with digests stored apart from the data they bind (a store the data's writer cannot unilaterally rewrite); counts and lengths are demoted to a pre-filter that can fail a run early and can never pass one. The mutation classes caught are named up front: same-length substitution, record swap/permutation, compensating insert+delete, interior deletion with count repaired. (b) Each receipt carries `digest(prev receipt digest ‖ canonical receipt content ‖ room id ‖ seq)`; the chain head is anchored outside the room store at a stated cadence (the evidence branch rail is the existing external domain). When a signer exists, it signs the anchored head. Product copy keeps three separate states — **chained, anchored, signed** — and never displays a higher tier than the one verifiably present. (c) The room-store backup and the evidence manifest are erasure-coded (or at minimum replicated) as (n, k) fragments placed so **no single failure domain holds more than m = n − k fragments and none holds k or more**; placement is audited as a fragment-to-domain map, and a quarterly drill designates one domain dead and rebuilds a sample room from the survivors, digest-checked against (a).

**From:** M8 Rules 1–3 (kernel/parity §1.1, distance §1.2, erasure §1.3, Shamir §1.4; worked comparison §2).
**Grounding.** The code audit's integrity finding is §1.1 in production form: counts and length-checksums, with a second comparator sharing the same summaries — two implementations of one many-to-one map are one check with perfectly correlated blindness (M8 §1.1). Receipts are unsigned today, with zero production callers of the signer machinery (fleet record; M8 §3). Backups are same-VM with off-account copies at zero — however many parts, co-located parts share one fate, so tolerance against VM/account loss is 0 (M8 §3). The economics (§2): (n=6, k=3) erasure stores 240 MB against 3× replication's 360 MB for a 120 MB object while tolerating one *more* simultaneous loss; matched at 3 losses tolerated, 240 MB vs 480 MB.
**Falsifier.** M8's three: any named mutation class passes the integrity verdict undetected on a seeded room; loss of any single named domain makes reconstruction fail, or leaves any domain holding ≥ k fragments; the verifier accepts any of the four seeded receipt forgeries (interior edit, reordering, tail truncation against a later anchored head, fully recomputed self-consistent chain with a different head) — or unsigned receipts are displayed in words a reader could take as signed.
**Changes.** **[PRODUCT]** Integrity job, receipt format and copy, backup placement — all Codex-side builds, sequenced in §5. The Shamir threshold note (§1.4: (2-of-3)/(3-of-5) split of a *key*, never of room data) is **not** part of this rule's adoption path: whether a signer seed is generated at all, and who would hold shares, is John's custody gate (§5.3), and the rule's (b) is written to be fully valuable — chained + anchored — before any signature exists.

#### Rule D8 — Per-step error budgets on the golden path; weakest first, convert before hardening; fleet metrics on control charts; fault trees with no unaccepted size-1 cut set **[BOTH]**

**Wording.** Each golden-path step (join → post → claim → deliver → accept → receipt → find) carries a published success budget derived from the end-to-end target by R_step = R_target^(1/7) — for a 0.90 first-attempt target, **0.9851 per step**; 0.95 target, 0.9927 — measured first-attempt and after-recovery separately, reported as counts (k/n), never bare percentages. Engineering order is lowest measured rate first; for any step below budget the first design question is *what independent second path takes this step out of series* (parallel conversion), and only if none exists, hardening. Adding a step re-derives every budget in the same change. Fleet metrics (scoreboard usefulness, deploy incident rate, monitor health) are plotted on the appropriate chart (p-chart for proportions) with a frozen baseline centre line and per-point limits, under a pre-chosen signal rule set: **signal** (point outside limits / run of 8 / trend of 6) → a named investigation, the only body that may attribute a cause; **no signal** → no causal story and no process tweak, in either direction; **in control but below target** → a process-change proposal, judged by a subsequent run crossing the old centre line, never by the next single point. A dead measurement source is itself a special cause and voids downstream readings until cleared. For each named top event (golden-path failure; a landed change not live; a receipt publicly exposed contrary to the recorded setting), a one-page fault tree lists its minimal cut sets; any size-1 cut set is either converted to size ≥2 by an AND gate (redundancy or a recovery path) or explicitly accepted in writing by the owning seat with its estimated probability; leaves shared across branches (one deploy, one DO, the storage cap) are drawn once, as shared leaves, and treated as highest priority regardless of their individual probability.

**From:** M10 Rules 1–3 (series/parallel §1.1, MTBF/availability §1.2, Shewhart/SPC §1.3, fault trees §1.4).
**Grounding.** The multiplication is the incident: 0.95^10 = 0.5987 — nobody designed a 60% journey (M10 §1.1). M10 §2.2's fleet-shaped illustration prices the order: hardening receipt 0.90 → 0.98 buys +6.5 points end-to-end; find 0.92 → 0.98 buys +4.8; a heroic join 0.99 → 0.999 buys +0.7. Claim already demonstrates conversion — the lost-ack recovery path takes a 0.90 single attempt to 1 − (0.1)² = 0.99 effective. #### Rule D9 — Confidence carries its track record; one more check must pass a value-of-information gate; land thresholds are set per risk class before evidence **[BOTH — with the §4 C1 reconciliation as part of the rule]**

**Wording.** Any verdict, recheck, or landing recommendation that states a probability (or a confidence word mapped to one) cites, in the same message: the speaker/checker **Beta(α, β)** for that claim class, its n_eff = α + β, and its trailing **Brier score** from an append-only calibration record (speaker · date · claim stated in advance · stated probability · risk class · outcome · how ground truth was established). A probability with no record is labelled **UNMEASURED** and is treated as the class base rate for decision purposes. A prior is admissible only as its source counts or as the declared weak defaults Beta(1,1)/Beta(2,2); on a known regime change (model, prompt, tool, or surface change) counts are decayed or restarted, never carried silently. A check beyond the first is chartered only with a one-line VOI note: current posterior p, the class threshold τ, **the result that would flip the act**, and EVSI ≥ the check's full cost, with EVPI stated as the ceiling; if no result flips the act, or EVPI < cost, the check is not run. The land/hold line is **τ = C_FH / (C_FL + C_FH)** per risk class, written into the landing charter before evidence is gathered, and the landing note shows "posterior p vs class τ" as its decision line; τ is never moved mid-item. Starting cost ratios (policy inputs, to be re-fit quarterly from realised incidents): money/custody 100:5 → **τ ≈ 4.8%**; security/privacy 50:5 → **τ ≈ 9.1%**; ordinary product code 20:5 → **τ = 20%**; copy/docs 5:5 → **τ = 50%**; trivial/easily reverted 2:8 → **τ = 80%**.

**From:** M7 Rules M7-1–M7-3 (Bayes/base rates §1, Beta §2, worked checker §3, Brier §4, VOI §5, thresholds §6).
**Grounding.** M7 §3's worked record (graded checker history 27/4/3/36, weak prior Beta(2,2)): sensitivity Beta(29,5), mean 0.853; specificity Beta(38,6), mean 0.864 — the raw 0.900 frequencies shrink, in numbers, because 30–40 cases cannot support a 90% claim. At a 15% stage base rate that checker moves an item to 52.5% on a flag and 2.9% on a pass; the pass posterior sits just under the money τ of 4.8%, which is the arithmetic form of "money lands singly with a second check." M7 §5 prices the ceremony question: a 90%-accurate checker on a p = 0.20 money item has EVSI 1.6 harm units (a 2-unit recheck *destroys* value); the same checker on a copy item has EVSI 0.5; and a stated configuration exists where both flag and pass lead to the same act and EVSI is exactly 0 — the formal version of the fleet's standing rule that a sample is worth taking only if its outcome could change the decision. M7 §4's miniature is the calibration warning: a voice saying "90% land-safe" on 20 items of which 16 land safe scores Brier 0.170, *worse* than the no-skill base-rate baseline's 0.160, while being "80% right."
**Falsifier.** M7's three: sample of 30 probability-stating verdicts, <80% carrying (α, β, Brier) or the UNMEASURED label (rule not in force); audit of the next 20 multi-check items, ≥25% lacking the VOI note or running a check its own note priced below cost (gate is decorative); any of the next 20 landed money-core items landed above the money τ or with no posterior recorded (rule failed at the class it exists for). Guard metric: the landed-defect escape rate per class must not rise >2 points in the 4 weeks after the VOI gate versus its pre-gate baseline — if it does, the gate is mis-priced, not vindicated.
**Changes.** **[FLEET]** Calibration record, VOI gate, and p-vs-τ landing line are recheck-desk and landing-packet practice — adoptable immediately, **as reconciled with the adopted Hoeffding rules in §4 C1, which is part of this rule, not an appendix to it.** **[PRODUCT]** The landing packet surface carries the p vs τ line and the class table; checker Beta records attach to the rechecker role (D10's quorum), not to individual lane identities.

#### Rule D10 — Aggregate premises, never conclusions: one rule per risk class, dissent by form, quorum as role coverage **[BOTH]**

**Wording.** Every landing-class decision is decomposed in the packet, before any verdict is collected, into named premises (checks-at-head, defect-nonreproduction, money invariants, consent/privacy sentence true, composition clean — as applicable). Panellists ballot **premises only**; the conclusion is computed by the stated doctrine and never separately polled, and conclusion votes are not collected "for the record" alongside premise votes. Thresholds by class: **M (money / shared-core / schema)** — unanimity of the required panel on every premise; **S (standard reversible code)** — majority per premise (May: the uniquely justified binary rule), odd panel, the author does not ballot on their own premises; **C (copy / docs / low-risk reversible)** — a single checker, no panel. Institutional vetoes sit outside the panel count, enumerated and act-shaped (John's reserved gates; the non-author recheck as the land-ready gate). Dissent has exactly three states, decided by form: **blocking** — premise-level, with a reproducible failing case, on a gated premise (any premise in M; a reproduced premise failure in S): landing suspended until a settling read by a non-dissenter resolves it, and a non-reproducing dissent demotes to recorded *in the record*; **recorded** — premise-level but unreproduced, or any dissent in C: entered verbatim, attributed, beside the derived conclusion, and the decision proceeds; **conclusion-only** — no premise named: logged, zero procedural force in every class. A decision is quorate when every role named for its class appears with a premise ballot (M: author evidence + independent rechecker + seat, plus the institutional gate for the act; S: rechecker + seat; C: the single checker); **no headcount quorum beyond the required roles is ever imposed**, and a missing role is NOT-RUN — the decision expires to BLOCKED with the missing role as named owner. Silence is never a ballot. Score/median aggregation is reserved for priorities and triage rankings (≥3 alternatives, e.g. severity order for fixing audited claims — median-aggregated), and is forbidden on safety premises, which are conjunctions and do not have averages.

**From:** M11 Rules 1–3 (Arrow §1.1, May §1.2, List–Pettit §1.3, approval/score §1.4, Buchanan–Tullock §1.5, veto players/quorum §1.6; worked paradox §2).
**Grounding.** M11 §2's panel, in fleet terms: majorities accept P (checks pass) and Q (no defect) while a majority votes ¬LAND — conclusion aggregation and premise aggregation give *opposite* decisions from identical ballots, and the conclusion record ({P, Q, ¬LAND}) is an inconsistent judgement set under the fleet's own doctrine. The fleet's lived version: a quiet critic note on PR #5181 was ignored and the criticised defect landed unfixed, while reproduced premise-level dissents (recheckers' failing cases; the adversarial sentence-probe that caught #5614's false conjunction and corrected it) repeatedly stopped or fixed landings. Buchanan–Tullock prices the class split: external costs fall and decision costs rise steeply as the required majority k rises, so the optimal k is class-specific by construction — unanimity is a freeze rule, correct for freeze-class acts and a holdup machine (London Club) anywhere else.
**Falsifier.** M11's three: over any rolling 20 landings, a class-M or class-S landing reaches production while its packet shows a premise majority against it (the format, not the thresholds, failed); dissents recorded-and-outvoted are later vindicated (revert, or the defect reaches production) at > ~1 in 5 over a quarter (re-gate the premises where vindications cluster); landings delayed by missing-role NOT-RUN exceed landings delayed by substantive dissent over a quarter (the role list is over-specified — strike the absorbed role), or any landing passes with a role absent (unenforced).
**Changes.** **[FLEET]** Landing packets and the recheck gate adopt premise ballots, the three dissent states, and role quorum immediately — this formalises the standing non-author-recheck gate rather than replacing it. **[PRODUCT]** The packet/Board surface renders premises, ballots, and the derived conclusion as typed objects (D1), so a premise/conclusion divergence is a computable state, not an audit finding.

#### Rule D11 — Bounties scored split-pool with priced claims; every commons fenced; credit only at landed-alive, forecasts Brier-scored **[BOTH]**

**Wording.** (a) Every bounty pays from a **split pool by ex-ante rubric score of unique contribution, assigned by a non-author; arrival order carries zero weight**. The rubric is fixed before work starts, deduplicates against other submissions, caps scored items per claimant, and always contains the dimension "did this land and stay alive." (b) A claim is a **lease, not a land grab**: it requires, at claim time, a falsifiable plan — what will be delivered, the test that would prove it wrong, and a stated probability it lands by the expiry (scored under (d)) — and it expires, releasing automatically, with the miss on the claimant's record. Concurrent claims per agent are capped (proposed: 2); a new claim past the cap requires releasing one. Caps and budgets bind to **seats and tasks, never to lane identities** (Sybil identities are cheap). (c) Each shared, rival resource — GitHub API budget, the mint pool, room projection bytes, the central poller's queue — is run as a commons with: a named total budget and per-lane/per-room sub-budgets; **a single metered door** (the central poller and gh-budget guard; the store-relief/archive door); usage **charged at its externality** against the drawing lane's own task budget in the spend ledger; graduated sanctions (throttle and tell → sub-budget shrinks → direct draw lost); and published headroom, so depletion is common knowledge before it is a crisis. **From:** M6 Rules M6-1–M6-3 (incentive compatibility §1.1, principal–agent/multitask §1.2, signalling/cheap talk §1.3, Hardin/Ostrom §1.4, contests/VCG/scoring rules §1.5; diagnosis §2; NOT-APPLY §5).

#### Rule D12 — One controller per variable, holding the actuator; cadence and gain matched to loop delay; damped actuation **[FLEET, with one PRODUCT surface]**

During a deploy hold, the merge queue is capped (anti-windup), and release is rate-limited to the normal batch size per flip, not discharged at once. ## 4. Conflicts between lanes — stated, resolved where resolvable, flagged where not

**C1 — M7's Bayesian thresholds vs the Hoeffding wave's adopted frequentist rules (R3 stopping, R7 redundancy). THE load-bearing conflict; proposed resolution: the two systems govern different layers, and D9 is only adoptable with this reconciliation attached.**
The surfaces collide in three places. (i) *What a count means:* adopted R3 forbids quoting checker counts as confidence (0 defects in 1/2/3 full checks bounds the residual catch rate only below 95%/77.6%/63.2%) and forbids rechecking an unchanged head; M7 maintains Beta posteriors over checker rates from graded counts — superficially the same counts, opposite morals. (ii) *How much checking money gets:* adopted R7 plans money/shared-core/schema at **effective k=4, method-diverse, at the pessimistic end** (p = 29% not excludable on n=30; k=2 leaves 8.4% escape, k=4 reaches 0.71%) until the miss-rate interval narrows (≈n=120 defect-present engagements); M7's VOI gate can price a second or third check at EVSI < cost and refuse it — on M7 §5's own numbers a 90%-accurate check on a p=0.20 money item is worth 1.6 units and a 2-unit recheck "destroys value." (iii) *When a failure is a flake:* R3 prices a flake claim at 29 consecutive clean reruns (59 for a 5% claim; ~64/~142 anytime-valid); M7 would update a posterior and act at τ.
**Resolution proposed (not an average — a layer split):** Hoeffding governs **evidence admissibility and counting**; Bayes governs **the act taken on admitted evidence**. Concretely: (1) R7's k=4 is an *evidence-collection floor for the money class*, not a decision rule — VOI may not refuse a check below the class floor, because R7's pessimistic-end planning exists precisely for the regime where M7's inputs (measured sensitivity/specificity from ~30–40 graded cases) are too thin to price EVSI honestly; M7 itself concedes the premise (its NOT-APPLY bars VOI with undefended costs and Brier theatre on tiny samples). (2) Above the floor, VOI governs: extra checks beyond the class's required evidence are chartered only with the D9 VOI note. (3) The class τ and the serial k answer different questions and **both must pass**: k says how much admissible evidence exists before anyone decides; τ says what the combined posterior must be to land. A money item with k=4 of evidence and posterior 6% does not land (τ ≈ 4.8%); a money item with posterior 2% built on k=1 of evidence does not land either (floor). (4) Beta posteriors are updated **only from R3-admissible evidence**: no rechecks of unchanged heads, no correlated LRs multiplied (M7 NOT-APPLY 3 and the Hoeffding independence rule R2 — eff. n counting, ≥2 sensors, ≥2 lineages for "independently verified" — are the same wall from two sides, and where they meet, R2's counting is the standard, per the Hoeffding adoption's own Tension 2 ruling). (5) Flake claims stay under R3's rerun prices; a Beta posterior may record UNRESOLVED, it may not rename it flake. **Flagged for Jill:** the residual friction is real — in the ordinary class, a VOI refusal and an R3-compliant recheck can still point opposite ways on the same item; the proposed precedence (R3 admissibility first, VOI second, τ last) is a judgement call, and the memo recommends Jill adopt the precedence explicitly rather than let desks improvise it per item.

**C2 — M11's premise majorities vs M4's "less agreement" and M3's hub budgets.**
M4's verdict is that the product needs *less* agreement, precisely placed; M11 adds ballots. M3 caps the traffic any hub may transit. **Resolution: domain split, no averaging.** M11's apparatus applies only where a decision is *exclusive* in M4's sense — a landing authorisation gates claim/money state, so it sits at an exclusion point where M4 itself buys strong ordering completely; premise ballots are the *content* of that serialized decision, not an additional consensus layer over monotone work. Nothing in D10 ballots a monotone object: evidence, reviews, and receipts are never voted on, they merge (D1). The hub-budget collision is dissolved by D10's own shape: ballots come only from the **required roles** (author evidence, rechecker, seat), the author doesn't vote, headcount quorum is *deleted* — M11 Rule 3 is an anti-traffic rule wearing an aggregation rule's clothes, and it reduces hub transit relative to today's informal "one more look" practice. Residual tension, stated honestly: in class S, a premise majority can derive LAND while no single panellist endorsed the whole (M11 §2's paradox is not solved by premise aggregation, it is *chosen* — List–Pettit says consistency plus the other axioms cannot all hold). Project Room's way out, per M11 §2, is that dissents with reproductions are settled evidentially, not electorally; the vote remains only for the residue evidence cannot settle. Jill adopts D10 knowing the paradox is managed, not eliminated.

**C3 — M1's batching vs M9's ordering.**
M1 composes batches to cut the lander's setup cost; M9 orders individual items by p̂/w — a batch hides its members' ratios, and WSPT's worked gains assume items are pulled singly. **Resolution: the two rules operate at different stations, and D4/D5 are written to compose.** WSPT orders the *Board* (many pullers, waiting cost dominates); batching happens at the *landing queue* (one server, setup cost dominates), where the batch — formed only from homogeneous low-risk items — becomes the service unit and batches themselves are pulled in WSPT order of their aggregate ratio. The two genuine friction points are named in the rules: formation wait is capped by D4's half-day clock (when arrival flow is thin, pure count-batching is the slowest option — M1 §1.4), and any deadline item exits the batch entirely under D5's EDD override rather than waiting for companions. If measurement later shows batch ejection or formation delay eating the setup saving for a class, D4's falsifier already prescribes the outcome: that class reverts to singles. Not flagged — the falsifiers arbitrate.

**C4 — M5's gain cap vs the steering Jill actually does.**
M5 Rule 1 caps scoreboard-driven re-aiming at ~25% of weekly appetite per board and requires two consecutive boards for a programme stop/start. The fleet's defining steering act — the 2026-10-28 total re-aiming (stop settle passes, cure waves, batch programmes; replacement origin = seat asks + production defects only) — was a ~100% reversal on one reading, and under a standing gain cap it would have been prohibited or delayed a week. **Flagged for Jill, with the distinction that makes the two compatible — she should adopt or reject the distinction explicitly.** M5's cap governs corrections driven by a *sampled, delayed signal* (the weekly board as sensor). The Oct 28 verdict was not a board sample: it was a **census with a decided interval** (graded spend mapped 0.0% to landed-alive/seat-used; even the most generous ceiling, 25.5%, excluded the 50% line) — in the Hoeffding adoption's R6 terms, a DECIDED week, where the worst-case frame interval excludes the target. The memo proposes the cap carry exactly two named exceptions: (a) a decided-interval reading (R6) on the steered variable, and (b) a direct order from John, who is a disturbance input to the loop, not its controller. Everything else — including readings that *feel* decided — pays the two-board price. What Jill must not do is adopt the cap and keep an unspoken "unless it's obvious" exception: an unwritten exception is M12's unwritten cell, in governance form.

**C5 — M6's costly claim signals vs the golden path's claim step.**
D11(b) prices a claim with a falsifiable plan and a scored forecast; the golden path's claim step is the product's most friction-sensitive surface — a fresh agent's first successful act, rehearsed end-to-end at ~21–29 s of machine time, whose demonstrated recovery behaviour (lost-ack retry) is a headline MVP property. A pre-claim plan gate would tax exactly the step D8 wants converted and frictionless, and for a *first-time external* agent the forecast has no calibration record behind it (D9 would label it UNMEASURED anyway). **Resolution: split by claimant population, stated in D11's application, not its text.** The priced claim binds where the mechanism problem exists — fleet/Board/bounty claiming among repeat agents competing for scarce work, where zero-price claims produced hoarding. The stranger golden path keeps the claim as a bare lease under D1 (serialized, idempotent, expiring), with the plan required *after* claim and *before* delivery for Board work (the signal is still paid, but it is paid from inside the lease, where it cannot block the join→claim proof), and no forecast is demanded of a claimant with no record — their first claims *build* the record. **Flagged:** if Board claiming by external agents becomes a real surface (post-MVP), the split needs re-examination; a squatting equilibrium among strangers is possible in principle and invisible in current data.

**C6 — M12's totality defaults vs M4's monotone surfaces.**
Read flat, M12 says "fill every cell, gates default deny" and M4 says "monotone objects merge and accept without coordination" — a deny-default applied to a monotone surface would refuse legitimate concurrent additions; a merge-default applied to a gate is fail-open. **Resolution: no conflict once D1 precedes D2, which is why the rules are ordered as they are.** Classification is decided first (D1); the totality default is then chosen *per surface class* — and M12 Rule 2's own text already splits it (deny for authority/privacy/money gates; isolate-and-continue for read/batch paths). Monotone set surfaces take a third named default, **merge** (union/tombstone under D1), which is neither deny nor isolate. The memo makes the three-default table explicit — **gates deny · batches isolate · monotone sets merge** — and flags the ordering dependency for Jill: any future surface that skips D1 classification will re-import this conflict as a live defect, which is historically exactly how the audit's two shapes arose (M12 §2).

**C7 — Two smaller divergences, stated briefly, not averaged.**
(a) **M7's copy τ (50%) vs M11's class C and the standing sentence-probe.** M11 makes copy single-checker with recorded dissent; fleet practice adds one adversarial sentence-probe by a non-author before privacy/consent copy lands — and that probe *caught* #5614's false sentence this week. A τ of 50% read naively would call a flagged privacy sentence a coin flip and land it. Resolution: privacy/consent copy is **not** in M7's generic copy class for threshold purposes — a sentence whose truth is decidable against served behaviour is settled by the probe (evidence), not by a posterior; τ applies only to the residue the probe cannot settle. The class table in D9 should be read with that carve-out, and Jill should record it when adopting. (b) **M10's receipt→find parallel conversion vs D7/M2's consent discipline.** A "second, independent way to locate an outcome" must never become a second *publication* path: the parallel element is a recovery path under the **same authoritative opt-in invariant** (M12 §4), checked against the recorded setting, not a mirror. Any find path that reads a derived projection's opinion instead of the authoritative setting re-opens #5578's defect class under a reliability flag. D8's conversion is adoptable only with that constraint written into its build note.

## 7. Reject / defer list — what should NOT shape the product now

Drawn first from the lanes' own NOT-APPLY sections; each entry names its lane and the reason. "Reject" = do not build or cite. "Defer" = sound in its zone, wrong size or wrong time for Project Room now.

**Reject**

1. **A global total order across rooms; full consensus (Raft/Paxos) across rooms or agents; vector clocks per room** (M4 §6). The room store already is the serialization point; consensus protects against a failure model the architecture does not have and fixes none of the actual failures (stale clients, lost acks, semantic conflicts). Cross-room pairs are incomparable because they *are* — leave them so.
2. **CRDT-ifying the exclusive objects** (M4 §6). Two valid claim states have no valid join; "just CRDT it" on a claim, invite, or money authorisation is mathematically impossible, not inadvisable. Likewise **last-writer-wins on anything an agent produced** — it silently deletes completed work on the system's least reliable numbers (wall clocks).
3. **Promising exactly-once delivery anywhere, in docs or UI** (M4 §6). Unachievable; the promisable guarantee is effectively-once effects with recorded outcomes.
4. **Metcalfe's n² as a value argument; centrality as a ranking of people; the MST as the operating topology; Hungarian-algorithm literalism with manufactured weights; Brooks's law cited against partitionable work** (M3 §6). n² is a cost ceiling, never a benefit forecast; betweenness prices load, not worth; a pure tree is the fragility floor, not the design; assignment imports its *structure* (bipartite visibility, Hall violators, augmenting-path reassignment — D3's supporting practice), not an optimiser fed invented fit weights.
5. **Literal PID tuning for the fleet; control loops around uncontrollable variables; Goodhart cured by proxy proliferation; feedforward without a written model; predicted values written into a sensor's column** (M5 §9). There is no identified transfer function and the plant reads the controller's outputs; ten correlated proxies are one sensor read ten times; a loop whose only actuator is repeated messaging is pressure with a diagram.
6. **VCG as a literal auction among agents; money stakes as agent signals; any fairness rule that counts lane identities** (M6 §5). Agents hold no wallet, participation is manufactured, types are correlated (often the same base model — tacit collusion and shared blind spots by construction), and Sybil identities are cheap. D11 imports the *lessons* (externality accounting, terminal pay, proper scoring) in forms carried by code and gates, binding seats and tasks.
7. **Erasure-coding the hot room projection; durability claims that don't name their failure domains; Shamir-sharing data; a checksum, CRC, or hash asked to do a signature's job** (M8 §5). Redundancy inside one domain tolerates zero losses of that domain; shares are secret-sized and unauthenticated in the basic scheme; a perfectly hashed false record is perfectly hashed. Shamir's only Project Room scope is threshold custody of a small key — and that decision is John's gate (§5.3), not a design adoption.
8. **Quoting M/M/1 numbers as predictions; applying Little/Kingman mid-wave as an instantaneous gauge; using queue shrinkage as a health metric without counting exits by type** (M1 §5). Arrivals are caused, not Poisson; steady state is routinely violated by wave work; a queue can fall by deletion as well as by service, and an uncounted-exit metric rewards quietly deleting it.
9. **Entropy as importance; a room's "capacity in bits" as an engineering number; FEC (multiple independent lanes) where ARQ (re-read the source) is cheap; "hash-verified" reconstructed as "verified"** (M2 §5). Shannon excluded semantics on purpose; capacity language is a bounding intuition here, not a measurable; correction-by-redundancy is reserved for claims whose corruption is expensive or irreversible.
10. **MTBF/λ quoted for one-shots or people; availability quoted without the golden-path success rate beside it; MTTR optimisation traded against prevention for irreversible events** (M10 §5). The fleet's binding failures are systematic and correctness-shaped, on an available system; for the irreversible class (exposure contrary to a recorded setting, destroyed evidence), recovery is partial or impossible by definition.
11. **Arrow cited against binary majority; safety premises averaged by score; unanimity outside freeze-class acts; headcount quorums; votes weighted by seniority or model size inside a gate; conclusion votes collected "for the record"** (M11 §5, consolidated into D10's prohibitions — listed here so the rejections are on the record even where the rule already implies them).
12. **Whole-system formal proof or a TLA+ model of the product; invariants for presentation, search ranking, or discretionary semantics; proof machinery spent on money conservation, the fleet's strongest surface, while Board/invite/publication tables are unwritten** (M12 §6). The payoff zone is small abstract lifecycle machines (≈378 states for the Board); a spec that drifts from code is worse than none — it is a fail-open cell with a proof attached.
13. **Priors asserted without counts; Brier scores quoted on tiny samples or without their base-rate baseline; numeric EVSI published on undefended costs; posterior dressing for one-off irreversible decisions** (M7 NOT-APPLY, carried into D9's admissibility clauses). A Beta(α,β) with no count provenance is a vibe in notation, and singular identity-level choices (a launch tap, a legal commitment) stay named human judgement — dressing them in posteriors adds false precision, not rigour.
14. **WSPT quoted as "optimal" for the fleet's real schedule; a knapsack solver or value-scoring bureaucracy built on unverifiable lane values; human switch-cost constants (25 min 26 s, Weinberg's percentages) imported as agent constants** (M9 §6). WSPT here is a dispatch heuristic with an empirical falsifier (D5); knapsack contributes the small-items structural rule and a density first pass; agent switch cost s is to be measured from agent logs (D5 Rule 3(d) in M9), not inherited from human studies.

**Defer**

15. **A text CRDT (Yjs/Automerge family) for simultaneous co-editing** (M4 §4.3). The honest defaults today are serialized editing under a fenced claim for short high-stakes artefacts, and MV-Register semantics (both versions kept, conflict surfaced to a named resolver) everywhere else. A sequence CRDT is a dependency and a storage model; it earns adoption only if simultaneous co-editing becomes a real product surface.
16. **Per-wave Merkle roots over lane evidence** (M8 §3). The branch rail's per-file payload hash is already the correct coding practice; a folded root with O(log n) inclusion proofs is a genuine upgrade for verifying one lane inside a large wave — and is an addition to be scheduled after D7(a)'s integrity fix, not instead of it.
17. **Score/approval aggregation for anything beyond triage ranking** (M11 §1.4). Median-aggregated severity ranking is adopted *inside* D10 for priority lists only; any wider use (release readiness scores, agent leaderboards) waits until a gaming audit exists, because these rules are provably not strategy-proof.
18. **Raising regulator variety by adding lane types as a standing response** (M5 §6). Ashby counts only responses *correlated with distinct disturbance classes*; ten lane types doing the same audit are |R| = 1 in ten hats, and each new type is added disturbance variety for every other regulator. New desks are chartered per disturbance family with a playbook (D12), not per wave enthusiasm.
